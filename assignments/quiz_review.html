1 Quiz question review

Quiz 0

Aassume that \(y_n = {\beta^{*}}_0 + x_n {\beta^{*}}_1 + \varepsilon_n\) for scalar \(x_n\) and some fixed \({\beta^{*}}_0\) and \({\beta^{*}}_1\). Assume that

  • The residuals \(\varepsilon_n\) are IID with \(\mathbb{E}\left[\varepsilon_n\right] = 0\) and \(\mathrm{Var}\left(\varepsilon_n\right) = \sigma^2\).
  • The regressors \(x_n\) are IID with \(\mathbb{E}\left[x_n\right] = \mu\) and \(\mathrm{Var}\left(x_n\right) = \nu^2\).
  • The residuals are independent of the regressors.

You should be able to analyze quantities involving \(x_n\), \(\varepsilon_n\), and \(y_n\) in terms of the fixed quantities \({\beta^{*}}_0\), \({\beta^{*}}_1\), \(\mu\), \(\sigma\), and \(\nu\).

  • Be able to compute all the conditional variances and expectations you can think of
  • Be able to apply the LLN and CLT, including recognizing when the CLT does not converge
  • Be able to apply the LLN to random matrices and their inverses, including recognizing when the matrix is or is not invertible.

In addition, you should be able to

  • Write out the \(\boldsymbol{X}\), \(\boldsymbol{Y}\), and \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\) matrices for simple linear regressions
  • Write out the OLS estimator \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\) in terms of these quantities
  • Be able to use the LLN and CLT to evaluate the limits of
    • \(\frac{1}{N} \boldsymbol{X}^\intercal\boldsymbol{X}\)
    • \(\frac{1}{N} \boldsymbol{X}^\intercal\boldsymbol{Y}\)
    • \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \boldsymbol{X}^\intercal\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\)
  • Compute \(\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\vert \boldsymbol{X}\right]\) and \(\mathrm{Cov}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\vert \boldsymbol{X}\right)\) in terms of \(\mathrm{Cov}\left(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\right)\), and plug in for particular values of \(\mathrm{Cov}\left(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\right)\).

In additition, you should be able to do all of the above for the special case when there is no \(x_n\) and there is only a constant.

Quiz 1

Suppose we observe data points of the form \((y_n, s_n, a_n)\), where \(a_n\) is a categorical variable and \(s_n\) is either a continous variable or another categorical variable.

You should be able to

  • Write the values of the \(\boldsymbol{X}\) matrix for a one-hot encoding of \(a_n\)
  • Write the values of the \(\boldsymbol{X}\) matrix for interactions between \(s_n\) and \(a_n\)
  • Understand the meaning of interactions (fitting a different slope for each level)
  • Write the regression involving the one-hot encoding with and without a constant term, and undersatnd how the interpretation of the coefficients change

Additionally, for all of these combinations, you should be able to do everything from Quiz 0.

Quiz 2

As a starting point, you should be able to do everything for Quiz 0 with a generic vector \(\boldsymbol{x}\) rather than a set of one or two scalars.

You should be familiar with our four assumptions:

  • Normality
  • Homoskedasticity
  • Heteroskedasticity
  • Machine learning (IID)

I’ll give you what these assumptions are, but you need to be able to understand and apply the definitions.

When we assume that \(y_n = {\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}}^\intercal\boldsymbol{x}_n + \varepsilon_n\), for each of our four assumptions, with enough hints you should be able to do everything from quiz 0, now in terms of \({\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}}\).

For testing, you should be able to analyze the t-statistic for a null of the form \({\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}}_k = 0\) for some component \(k\).

  • Know what the distribution of the t-statistic is under the null for normality (all you need is to understand its properties, you don’t need to memorize anything)
  • Know why you would use an F-statistic, and what its distribution is under the normal assumption (all you need is to understand its properties, you don’t need to memorize anything)
  • Know the distribution of the t-statistic for large N under non-normal assumptions
  • Relatedly, understand how to compute the sandwich covariance and how it is different from the standard covariance
  • Be able to compute the level of a given test by applying the above results under the null
  • Be able to compute the power of a test against a particular alternative by plugging in an alternative \({\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}}\)

The question will take the form of undertaking one or more of these tasks under a particular tractable choice of \(\boldsymbol{x}\).

Quiz 3

This question will be about a one-dimensional example \(y\sim z\) of a machine learning problem, focusing on squared loss.

You should be able to

  • Write out the \(\boldsymbol{X}\) matrix for a zeroth order spline basis of \(z\)
  • Be able to plot the basis functions
  • Understand intuitively what would change with higher-order splines (no need to know the exact formulas)
  • Understand the relationship between the zeroth order spline, partitions of the domain of \(z\), and one-hot encodings
  • Know what the fitted value \(\hat{y}\) is in each partition and be able to plot them (note: this is a generalization of quiz 1)
  • Understand intuitively the costs and benefits of having more or fewer partitions

Additionally, you should be able to do all of Quiz 0 for ridge regression. You should also have an intuitive understanding of the costs and benefits of having a large or small ridge parameter.

Quiz 4

This question will be about omitted variable bias in a problem with two regressors which may be correlated with one another. You will need the tools from Quiz 0 for two scalar-valued regressors.

You should be able to

  • Relate assumptions about real-world relationships between the regressors to statistical assumptions about their correlation, and use this to derive the limiting behavior of the components of the OLS estimator
  • Relate assumptions about real-world causal relationships between the regressors and the valued of coefficients in an assumed true data generating process
  • Under a misspecified regression, derive the limiting value of the OLS regression coefficient, especially to show when and why it is an incorrect estimator of the assumed true coefficient
  • Relate the real-world consequences of omitted variable bias to the mis-estimation of the OLS coefficients under misspecification.

## Quiz 0 {-}

Aassume that $\y_n = \betastar_0 + \x_n \betastar_1 + \res_n$ for scalar $\x_n$
and some fixed $\betastar_0$ and $\betastar_1$. Assume that

- The residuals $\res_n$ are IID with $\expect{\res_n} = 0$ and $\var{\res_n} = \sigma^2$.
- The regressors $\x_n$ are IID with $\expect{\x_n} = \mu$ and $\var{\x_n} = \nu^2$.
- The residuals are independent of the regressors.

You should be able to analyze quantities involving $\x_n$, $\res_n$, and $\y_n$
in terms of the fixed quantities $\betastar_0$, $\betastar_1$, $\mu$, $\sigma$, and $\nu$.

  the matrix is or is not invertible.

- Write out the $\X$, $\Y$, and $\resv$ matrices for simple linear regressions

case when there is no $\x_n$ and there is only a constant.
